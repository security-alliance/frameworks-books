\chapter{If it breaks}

Speed beats dignity. A seed you ``might'' have shown is
a seed you showed.

\section{Seed or key exposed}

New wallet, on a device you trust. New seed, offline
backup, then move. Sweep tokens, NFTs, and anything that
needs a separate transfer. Revoke approvals on the old
address after the value is gone, from the new setup,
not by lingering on the burned one.

Do not reuse the old seed, the old device, or the old
browser profile. Familiar is how you get hit twice.

\section{Signer seat burned}

Tell the other signers on a channel that is not the one
you fear. Remaining quorum replaces the address. If you
cannot prove the old key is yours because it is gone,
they verify you some other way: video, known social,
whatever you documented in advance. Inventing that
process during the incident is how a lookalike joins.

\section{Device looks wrong}

Stop signing. Do not factory-reset away the evidence
until you have a replacement that actually works. Treat
secrets that were on an unlocked phone as gone.

Hardware that was out of your sight, mailed oddly, or
offered as a gift is not your hardware.

\section{This is the wrong book}

If the problem is a person with a wrench, a kidnapping,
or a family threat, close this and open Physical
Security. Moving funds while someone is still in the
room can make the violence worse. Life first.

If the problem is travel, hotel Wi-Fi, or a border
laptop, the travel OpSec companion is closer.

\section{After}

Look after the human who got hit before you demand a
timeline. Then write down what was actually used: the
site, the approval, the device, the copy of the seed.
Close that hole. Rotate anything that sat next to it.
Tell the people who hold the other copies.

You do not get to keep the old setup because rebuilding
is annoying. The attacker already has that setup.
