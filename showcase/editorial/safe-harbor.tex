\chapter{Safe Harbor}

Safe Harbor lets a protocol pre-authorize whitehats to
rescue funds during an active exploit, under published
scope and rules. It is not a bug bounty, and it is not
cover for the person who started the hack.

Without it, whitehats watch a drain they could have
stopped because the lawyers have not answered yet. That
is how Nomad sat open for hours.

\section{Insights}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Only live or imminent exploits. Disclosure
  before the hack is still a bounty problem.
  \item Whitehats return funds to your recovery
  addresses. Bounty terms are set in advance.
  \item The initiator who ``rescues'' with a second
  account is still a blackhat. No bounty.
  \item Do not rewrite the legal standard. Set scope:
  contracts, chains, recovery addresses, KYC, bounty
  cap.
\end{itemize}

\section{Quick wins}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Read the eligibility checklist on the site.
  \item Publish recovery addresses you actually control.
  \item Cap the Safe Harbor bounty at or below the
  existing bounty so faking a rescue does not pay.
  \item Adopt on-chain. Uniswap and others already did.
\end{enumerate}
