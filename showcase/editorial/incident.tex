\chapter{Incident management}

Decide who leads, how you communicate, and what to
freeze before the incident. Web3 windows are short.
Many losses are irreversible.

Plans written under stress lose to plans practiced in
calm. SEAL 911 exists for when you need a war room.
Have the number before you need the number.

\section{Insights}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Pause paths and signer reachability are the
  first hour. Blog posts are not.
  \item Unconfirmed claims in public make the second
  incident.
  \item Playbooks on the site cover drainers, DPRK,
  malware, and front-end or DNS takeovers. Use them.
  \item Forensics needs logs you kept on purpose. You
  will not invent them at 3am.
\end{itemize}

\section{Quick wins}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Name an incident lead and a backup. Write the
  phone tree.
  \item Know how to pause, and who can. Dry-run it.
  \item Save SEAL 911 and your legal contact offline.
  \item Afterward, write what you will do differently.
  Then change the system, not only the doc.
\end{enumerate}
