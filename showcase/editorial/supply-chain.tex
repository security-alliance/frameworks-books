\chapter{Supply chain}

Your software is only as secure as its weakest
dependency. Know what you depend on. Verify integrity.
Have a plan for when something in the chain is
compromised.

Attackers do not need your Solidity. They need the npm
package your front end imports, the CDN that serves it,
or the contractor who opened a PR.

\section{Insights}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Web3 chain is longer: compilers, wallet
  connectors, RPC, oracles, bridges, hardware.
  \item Lockfiles are evidence. Regenerating them
  casually is how a swap hides.
  \item Typosquatting still works because people install
  from a README they skimmed.
  \item Human supply chain is the DPRK chapter. Hardware
  is the wallet chapter. Do not mix the runbooks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Quick wins}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pin versions. Commit the lockfile. Diff it in
  review.
  \item Inventory direct dependencies and who owns each
  one.
  \item Restrict who can publish the front end. Integrity
  hashes if you can.
  \item When a library is hijacked, assume users already
  loaded it. Incident, not a quiet bump.
\end{enumerate}
