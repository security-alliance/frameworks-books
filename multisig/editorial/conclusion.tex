\chapter{Conclusion}

Operational security for a protocol set is not
about collecting M signatures. It is about making
it harder for one bad day to rewrite the
contracts, and easier to pause when you must.

Separate the powers. Delay what can wait. Verify
the effect, not the chrome.

You can refuse to join a theater quorum. That is
part of the job.

When you rotate, offboard, or survive an incident,
write down what changed. The next signer should
not have to reverse-engineer your memory.

Keep the footprint of each key small, act on
purpose, and do not let the UI become the
authority.

The live pages move. This book is a companion.
If they disagree, check the date, then think.
Do not skip the decode either way.
