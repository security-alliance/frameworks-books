\chapter{Do this next}

If the rest of the book stayed abstract, this chapter is
the order of work. Numbers are examples. Replace them
with limits that match your balances.

\section{Rough maturity}

\textbf{Basic / high risk.} Single-factor cold, no daily
limits, ad-hoc signing, loud public profile.

\textbf{Intermediate.} Multisig or MPC on treasury, some
limits, a separate signing device, some staff who have
been told the safety-first rule.

\textbf{Advanced.} Keys in more than one place, real role
separation, time-locked reserve, drills, someone watching
the wallets.

\section{This week}

\subsection{Individual}

Take wallet apps, exchange apps, seeds, and screenshots
off the daily phone and laptop.

Put a hard daily limit on hot funds. The source example
is under \$10k equivalent. Move the rest off that device.

Stand up one small wallet you can surrender without
lying, and only then consider a duress PIN that silently
alerts someone who already agreed to be that someone.

Walk the house and the office. If a stranger would see a
hardware wallet or a recovery sheet, it moves today.

\subsection{Organization}

List every active signer.

Confirm that no single person can move more than a small
slice of treasury without a second human. The source
example is 1--5\%.

Brief the people who would be in the room. Safety first.
No improvising resistance from a Slack thread.

\section{This quarter}

\subsection{Individual}

Split public identity from asset control: other email,
other SIM, other device.

Put cold storage in a scheme where keys are not in one
place.

Pick the trusted contact or the security service for a
panic path, and actually tell them.

\subsection{Organization}

Name the job of each tier. Hot, warm, cold. Automatic
limits.

Distribute signing by geography and by function, not by
who happens to have a Ledger in a drawer.

Write down how new operational keys are born and backed
up. Run that process once on purpose.

Put wallet and coercion training in onboarding, not in a
wiki nobody opens.

\section{Later}

\subsection{Individual}

Clean the obvious OSINT: address, family, travel, social.

If travel is part of the job, use a physical-security
shop for that, not a group chat.

Legal structure that hides ownership is a lawyer problem,
not a Twitter problem.

K\&R insurance that understands crypto, if the profile
warrants it. The useful part is often the negotiator,
not the PDF.

\subsection{Organization}

Quarterly tabletops: coercion, kidnapping, insider.

Watch treasury wallets for movement that does not match
the policy.

Retain people who already know this incident type.

Pay for tests against the technical path and against
staff, separately. One pentest that never leaves the
staging cluster will not find the signer who talks in
Ubers.
